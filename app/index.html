01

A pulse does not vanish

Release 1 tonne of CO2.

The emission takes an instant, we call it a "pulse". This pulse immediately increases the CO2 concentration in the atmosphere, but exchanges with oceans, soils, and vegetation then move parts of it at very different rates. Beyond this 500-year window, ocean–sediment chemistry and rock weathering continue the drawdown over tens to hundreds of thousands of years and longer; those processes are not represented here.

One curve, four fitted terms

Several timescales act at once.

03.2

Why remaining CO2 matters

A small change in the flow of energy.

Emission→ Atmospheric concentration→ Radiative forcing→ Climate response

CO2 absorbs and re-emits infrared radiation. Adding it reduces the rate at which energy escapes to space.

Incoming energy then exceeds outgoing energy. This difference is an energy imbalance. The climate warms, which increases energy loss to space and partly restores the balance.

04.2
