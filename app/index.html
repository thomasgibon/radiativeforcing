A pulse does not vanish

Release 1 tonne of CO2.

The emission takes a moment. Exchanges with oceans, soils, and vegetation then move parts of it out of the atmosphere at very different rates. Beyond this 500-year window, ocean–sediment chemistry and rock weathering continue the drawdown over tens to hundreds of thousands of years and longer; those processes are not represented here.

From presence to influence

Remaining CO2 changes the flow of energy.

Atmospheric burden is not yet radiative forcing. The gas's radiative efficiency translates each kilogram still aloft into a small change in Earth's global mean energy flow.

03

Why remaining CO2 matters

A small change in the flow of energy.

CO2 absorbs and re-emits infrared radiation. Adding it reduces the rate at which energy escapes to space.

Incoming energy then exceeds outgoing energy. This difference is an energy imbalance. The climate warms, which increases energy loss to space and partly restores the balance.
